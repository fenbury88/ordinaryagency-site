The admin jobs worth automating first

17 September 2026 · 2 min read · Mike Fenbury

When people hear “automation” or “AI” they tend to imagine something big and expensive. In a small business, the best wins are usually small and boring. They’re the jobs someone does the same way every day, copying information from one place to another.

Here’s where we’d look first.

Enquiries that get retyped

A web form comes in by email. Someone copies the name and number into a spreadsheet or CRM, then sends a “thanks, we’ll be in touch” reply. That whole chain can happen on its own the second the form is submitted, and nothing gets lost in an inbox over the weekend.

Quote and invoice follow-ups

Most businesses don’t chase quotes because nobody has time to remember. A simple rule that sends a friendly check-in a few days after a quote goes out, and stops the moment the customer replies, wins back work you were already close to winning.

Booking confirmations and reminders

If your team is texting customers the day before a job, that’s a reminder a system can send. It cuts no-shows and frees up the person who was doing it.

Reviews after a finished job

The best time to ask for a Google review is right after a job goes well. An automatic message with a direct link, sent when a job is marked complete, gets far more reviews than remembering to ask.

Where AI actually helps

AI is useful for the messy middle: summarising a long email thread, drafting a first reply, or pulling the key details out of a job description someone typed in a hurry. It works best with a person checking the result, not running unsupervised on anything that matters to a customer.

How to pick your first one

Ask your team one question: what do you copy and paste every day? The answer is usually your first automation. Keep it small, get it working, then do the next one.

If you’ve got a job in mind and want to know whether it can be automated, tell us about it. Some are a quick setup. Some aren’t worth it, and we’ll say so.
