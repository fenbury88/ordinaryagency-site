Fix your website before you pay for ads

17 September 2026 · 2 min read · Mike Fenbury

Every few weeks someone rings us wanting to “turn on Google Ads”. Fair enough. Ads work. But the first thing we do is open their website on a phone, because that’s where the ad money actually lands.

An ad doesn’t sell anything. It buys you a visitor. What happens next is the website’s job, and most small-business sites aren’t ready for it.

What we check first

Before we spend a dollar of anyone’s budget, we look at five things:

  • Can I call you in one tap? The phone number should sit at the top of every page on mobile, and it should be a link, not an image.
  • Do I know what you do in three seconds? “Welcome to our website” tells me nothing. “Emergency plumber, Perth northern suburbs” tells me everything.
  • Does the page match the ad? If the ad says “same-day glass repairs” and the link drops me on your home page, I have to go hunting. Most people won’t.
  • Does it load quickly on a phone? Slow pages lose people before they’ve read a word, and you still pay for the click.
  • Is there a reason to trust you? Real reviews, real photos of your work, an ABN, a local address. Stock photos of handshakes don’t count.

Why the order matters

If the site is leaky, ads just make the leak more expensive. You’ll see clicks in the report, no calls on your phone, and conclude that “ads don’t work for us”. Usually the ads were fine. The landing spot wasn’t.

Fixing the site first is cheaper than it sounds. Often it’s a clearer headline, a proper call button and a dedicated page for the one service you’re advertising. None of that needs a rebuild.

Then turn the ads on

Once the site can turn a visit into an enquiry, ads become a tap you can open and close. You can see what a lead costs, decide if it’s worth it, and put more in when it is.

If you’re not sure whether your site is ready, send it to us. We’ll tell you straight, even if the answer is “don’t spend money on ads yet”.
