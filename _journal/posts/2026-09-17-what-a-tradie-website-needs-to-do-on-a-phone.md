Picture who's looking for you. A burst pipe, a dead air conditioner, a sliding door that's come off its track. They're standing next to the problem with a phone in one hand. They are not settling in to read about your company history.

Your website has one job in that moment: make it easy to get hold of you. Here's what that looks like.

## Put the phone number where a thumb can reach it

A tap-to-call button, visible without scrolling, on every page. A sticky bar at the bottom of the screen works well. If someone has to find the contact page, copy the number and switch apps, you've given them three chances to ring someone else instead.

## Say where you work

"Servicing all Perth" is fine, but naming suburbs and regions is better. It reassures the person you'll actually come out, and it helps Google show you to people nearby.

## Show the work, not stock photos

Photos of real jobs you've done are the most convincing thing on a trade website. They don't need to be professional. Before-and-after shots from your own phone, taken in decent light, beat any image library.

## Keep forms short

Name, phone, suburb, and a box for "what's going on". Maybe a photo upload. Every extra field is another reason to give up. You can ask the rest when you call them back.

## Make it quick

People on mobile data in a carport won't wait for a heavy page to load. Oversized images, sliders and autoplay videos are the usual culprits. A fast, plain site will beat a slow, pretty one every time.

## Check it yourself

Pull out your phone, open your own website and pretend your hot water system just died. Can you ring yourself inside ten seconds? If not, that's the first thing to fix.
